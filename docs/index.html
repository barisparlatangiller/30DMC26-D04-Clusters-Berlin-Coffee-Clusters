    Method, data & limits

    How the clusters are made

    1 · Data. All OpenStreetMap objects tagged amenity=cafe in Berlin (Overpass export of 4 Nov 2026), as points in EPSG:25833.

    2 · Opening hours. Each café's opening_hours tag is parsed in your browser with opening_hours.js, the reference parser of the OSM community. There is one source field and no pre-computed hour columns: the state for the time you pick is calculated on the fly as open, closed or unknown. Missing, unparsable or explicitly “unknown” hours stay unknown and are never counted as closed.

    3 · Week. Days are evaluated for the reference week 9–15 November 2026, which has no public or school holidays in Berlin. Rules for holidays (PH) or special dates in the tag are therefore not shown.

    4 · DBSCAN. The open cafés are clustered with DBSCAN (Ester et al. 1996): a café is a core point if at least min cafés (itself included) lie within eps metres; clusters are chains of core points plus their neighbours, everything else is noise. Default 250 m / 5. Same parameters as scikit-learn, and the pipeline checks the browser version against it.

    5 · Colours follow a cluster through time: when the clock moves, a cluster keeps the colour of the cluster most of its cafés belonged to a moment before. Names come from the Ortsteil where most of its cafés are.

    Data quality & limits

    OSM opening hours are entered by volunteers and can be outdated. Being “open” here means the tag says so, not that the café is open today. The map is not a live check.

    Cafés are a mix of bakeries with seating, chains, specialty coffee and café-bars, because that is how they are tagged in OSM. Clusters show where cafés concentrate, not where good coffee is.

    DBSCAN tends to chain dense inner-city areas into one large cluster. The sensitivity table shows how cluster counts change with the settings.
